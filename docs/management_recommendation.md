# Management recommendation (draft)

Use the one-day, time-ordered compressed layout for the historical reporting store, and retain the one-day uncompressed hypertable for recent selective reads. Time-only compression reduced the cleaned baseline from 1,503.8 MB to 460.3 MB, a 3.27x ratio and 69.4% saved. With all 29 chunks compressed, fresh Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q5 medians improved by 3.8%, 4.5%, 42.9% and 22.5% against the same one-day uncompressed baseline. The measured chunk-compression cost was 12.84 seconds. The tradeoff is selected-meter Q4: time-only compression took 53.44 ms versus 2.50 ms on the uncompressed baseline. Meter segmentation reduced Q4 to 2.65 ms, close to the raw baseline, but saved less storage (65.0% versus 69.4%), took 43.61 seconds to compress, and raised Q1 to 240.33 ms. Keep frequent selective meter reads on the uncompressed table; consider meter segmentation if that workload becomes dominant.

For next-day planning, use the selected random-forest model as a decision-support forecast, not an automated dispatch instruction. On the 15 October 1,680-hour test run its MAE was 5.133 kWh and RMSE 7.147 kWh, compared with 42.708 kWh and 79.403 kWh for the previous-day baseline. The historical grid was 98.993% complete; missing intervals were kept missing, so reported actuals may understate consumption. The offline evaluation used finalized event-time data. A live forecast can be lower until delayed readings arrive; display data freshness and refresh forecasts as late readings are incorporated.

Shift demand in the observed weekday peaks through operational load management, and use the regional and customer-type totals to target outreach and capacity checks. Treat kW as instantaneous interval-average demand and kWh as consumed energy when sizing storage or communicating savings. Reassess recommendations with more seasons and a live-arrival replay before operational deployment.
