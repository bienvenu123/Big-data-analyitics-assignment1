# Management recommendation (draft)

Use the one-day, time-ordered compressed layout for the historical reporting store, and retain the one-day uncompressed hypertable for recent selective reads. Time-only compression reduced the cleaned baseline from 1,503.8 MB to 460.3 MB, a 3.27× ratio and 69.4% saved. With all 29 chunks compressed, Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q5 medians improved by 14.4%, 30.2%, 58.7% and 35.8% against the same one-day uncompressed baseline. The measured chunk-compression cost was 12.84 seconds. The tradeoff is the selected-meter Q4: time-only compression took 43.71 ms versus 2.38 ms on the uncompressed baseline. Meter segmentation reduced Q4 to 2.76 ms, about 94% faster than time-only compression and close to the raw baseline, but saved less storage (65.0% versus 69.4%) and took 43.61 seconds to compress. Keep frequent selective meter reads on the uncompressed table; consider meter segmentation if that workload becomes dominant.

For next-day planning, use the selected random-forest model as a decision-support forecast, not an automated dispatch instruction. On the 1,680 test hours its MAE was 5.13 kWh and RMSE 7.14 kWh, compared with 42.71 kWh and 79.40 kWh for the previous-day baseline. The historical grid was 98.993% complete; missing intervals were kept missing, so reported actuals may understate consumption. The offline evaluation used finalized event-time data. A live forecast can be lower until delayed readings arrive; display data freshness and refresh forecasts as late readings are incorporated.

Shift demand in the observed weekday peaks through operational load management, and use the regional and customer-type totals to target outreach and capacity checks. Treat kW as instantaneous interval-average demand and kWh as consumed energy when sizing storage or communicating savings. Reassess recommendations with more seasons and a live-arrival replay before operational deployment.
